# Snapshot copy buffers, November 11, 2026

This round reduces exporter allocation volume without a material change in full
CI latency. Baseline `dc010bb` includes previous-plan reuse; implementation
`7f14e51` gives each snapshot worker one reusable 32 KiB copy buffer. Previously,
every package allocated its own buffer. No source selection, copied bytes,
hashing, compression, package recipes, or worker tools change.
[Raw measurements and validation](ci-round17-2026-11-11.json) accompany this report.

## Allocation volume and standalone export

A CPU/allocation profile of prepared UPM identified per-package copy buffers as
the largest individual allocation site. Ten iterations of the exporter benchmark
per revision, using Go 1.26.5, four Go threads, prepared modules, and the compact
planning toolchain, measured:

| Measurement | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allocated bytes per export | 159,263,104 | 141,042,323 |
| Allocations per export | 498,814 | 498,198 |

The allocated-byte reduction is 11.4%, or 18.2 MB per export. These are allocation
counters, not peak-memory measurements. The benchmark source and full output
are retained in the JSON. Its fixed-order timings are not used for speed claims.

A separate comparison uses seven alternating trials plus warmups at each thread
count. Both executables use the same SDK and full toolchain; output replacement
is included. All 48 runs produce byte-identical `graph.json`, `evaluation.json`,
`scripts.nix`, and resolved toolchain metadata, including all source hashes.

| Go threads | Before | After | Reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.634 s | 0.631 s | 0.5% |
| 2 | 0.504 s | 0.496 s | 1.7% |
| 4 | 0.450 s | 0.444 s | 1.3% |

Raw `wait4` maximum-RSS observations are included for completeness; they do not
measure the combined peak memory of concurrent Go subprocesses.

## Complete warm CI

Seven alternating trials plus warmups force fresh dynamic planners with warm
tools and compiled Go outputs. Totals include outer evaluation and realization,
nested planning, and output forwarding. Sandboxing is enabled without fallback;
IFD, substituters, and forced substitutions are disabled. Nix uses four jobs and
two cores per job. Nested iets is static upstream PR #3 at
`69883a64bfb0d9a95dce253dae304878e07b076e`, with one worker and both caches and
fallback disabled. Every before/after comparison preserves the Go command output.

| Workload | Nested evaluator | Before | After |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| fixture | Nix | 0.865 s | 0.871 s |
| fixture | iets | 0.667 s | 0.660 s |
| UPM proxy | Nix | 3.369 s | 3.355 s |
| UPM proxy | iets | 2.983 s | 2.982 s |
| UPM prepared modules | Nix | 2.492 s | 2.472 s |

Changes range from 0.7% slower to 1.1% faster. Treat full CI latency as effectively
unchanged. The benefit is lower allocation volume, without additional dependencies.
These measurements exclude cold compilation and network transfers.

UPM is revision `35a76c282507fb942445c0ee98427b3d1ef772bc`, package `./cmd/upm`.

## Cache payload and validation

Complete fixture planner inputs gain 176 NAR bytes and shrink by 754 bytes in
fresh local zstd-level-3 binary caches, with unchanged path counts: 964 for
nested Nix and 909 for nested iets. This is effectively unchanged transfer size.
Worker toolchains and package outputs are identical.

Unit tests and vet pass. Existing snapshot tests compare copied hashes with Nix,
cover source modes and large files, and reject unsafe paths. The focused iets
suite passes fresh/cached equivalence, compact interfaces, early cutoff, embed
edits, new dependencies, invalid source, and empty-test validation. Outer iets
with one and four workers agrees with Nix. The complete platform/SDK CI matrix
remains the merge gate.

## CPU-limit experiment

A separate diagnostic tested planner `GOMAXPROCS` values of one, two, four, eight,
and the default on this 20-logical-CPU host. Five rotating-order trials plus
warmups covered one and four concurrent prepared-UPM planners, with and without
a previous plan. All 120 runs resolve to the same command output. Selected
median totals are:

| Concurrent planners | Previous plan | Default | Two threads | Four threads |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | no | 2.063 s | 2.103 s | 2.020 s |
| 1 | yes | 1.152 s | 1.209 s | 1.126 s |
| 4 | no | 4.079 s | 4.098 s | 4.075 s |
| 4 | yes | 1.931 s | 1.998 s | 1.891 s |

One and two threads slowed every case. Four or eight threads gave small gains,
with little improvement for four planners without a saved plan. This evidence
does not justify a new default CPU cap; the experiment remains diagnostic only.
Raw records and the temporary Nix patch are included in the JSON. These timings
come from a different session than the buffer comparison and must not be mixed.
